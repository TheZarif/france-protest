# Background: the slogan and the movement

*Compiled 26 Oct 2026 from press coverage and Wikipedia. Dates are 2026. Claims about who wrote the original message are **unverified**.*

## What the phrase is

**« Bismillah c'est les ténèbres »** combines the Arabic *bismillah* ("in the name of God", said by Muslims before beginning something) with *c'est les ténèbres* ("it's darkness"; in youth slang, "it's grim"). Read literally it is close to nonsense, and that is the joke.

It comes from a **screenshot of a Snapchat message** that circulated in early October. The message came from an account calling itself "Mamadou" that tried to join group chats where lycéens were organising school blockades. In clumsy slang it called for blockades and property damage. Students concluded the sender was an undercover police officer imitating, badly, how young people of North African heritage are imagined to speak. Activists described the message as a racist caricature. The police and the interior ministry have not commented, and the account's operator has not been independently identified. La Provence reported (16 Oct, unconfirmed) that the screenshot's geolocation pointed to an Aix-en-Provence police station.

Repeating the line became a way to **mock the infiltration attempt and the stereotype behind it**. It appeared on placards and in chants, parody videos and memes, and also served as an in-group warning ("if you see 'casseurs' who look like cops…").

## The movement

- **18 Sept:** lycée blockades begin in Créteil over overcrowded classes, run-down buildings and teacher absences. They spread nationwide.
- **13 Oct:** national day of action. 161,500 demonstrators according to the Interior Ministry, 480,000 according to student organisations.
- **17 Oct:** student protests merge with cost-of-living demonstrations. Some of the worst rioting and looting in years follows. Far-right figures blame Muslim youths, and Donald Trump claims the unrest is about "Islam wanting to take over".
